# Resume Tailor

Use this skill when preparing a job-specific resume.

## Workflow
1. Select only evidence supported by the canonical candidate profile.
2. Prioritize truthful JD-aligned keywords and accomplishments.
3. Rewrite summaries and bullets for relevance without changing factual meaning.
4. Preserve dates, employers, titles, education, and other fixed facts.
5. Select evidence by relevance and signal, not by keyword density alone.
6. Include current personal projects when they are relevant and demonstrable; label them explicitly as personal/current projects, never as employment.
7. For an employment gap, prefer a verified current project, professional development, or other verified activity over an unexplained blank period when it materially helps the target role.
8. When a project is included, use only repository/document evidence that can be demonstrated or explained by the candidate; do not invent architecture, deployment, users, metrics, or production usage.
9. Preserve enough supported evidence to produce a readable, substantively filled page; do not intentionally compress content into a sparse half-page output.
10. When the draft is underfilled, recover omitted supported P0/P1 proof first, then mechanism/ownership/decision/result detail, then the next-best differentiated role/project evidence, before adding generic skills or adjectives.
11. When the draft overflows, remove low-signal or redundant material before reducing typography or spacing.
12. Return an ATS-readable draft plus a trace of material edits, omitted evidence, project evidence used, and any genuine evidence gaps.

## Composition rules
- Target a full but readable one-page composition when a one-page resume is requested.
- Underfill is a quality failure, not a reason to add filler.
- Overflow is a quality failure, not a reason to shrink typography below the approved renderer bounds.
- Do not delete a relevant role or current verified project solely to create whitespace; compress or omit only when its evidence value is materially lower than competing evidence.
- Do not add generic skills, repeated keywords, coursework, weak awards, or invented metrics merely to fill space.
- Keep a short summary only when it adds a clear candidate thesis beyond the experience bullets.

## Rules
- Never add an unverified tool, responsibility, metric, credential, employer, project result, architecture, or deployment claim.
- Never convert external role research into candidate experience.
- Never convert an old/generated resume claim into verified evidence without explicit provenance.
- Do not optimize for keyword density at the expense of readability, evidence quality, or page composition.
